This work was born from a feeling that has accompanied me for years: living in a geography where war is part of everyday memory. My lived experience in southern Iran — houses still bearing the scars of war, and people who carry a constant readiness for danger within them — was the main source of inspiration. I turn to surrealism because our reality is often stranger than any dream. In this painting, I sought to portray the act of seeking refuge and solidarity amid insecurity: how fragile beings find calm beside one another when nowhere feels safe
